---
name: Center-of-gravity acceptance
description: Project rule for treating load-center position as a primary loading criterion.
---

For simple platforms, a loading plan should not be accepted merely because all parts fit and have geometric support. The weighted load center must be brought close to the physical platform center; a large rear or front offset is a warning and should drive a forward/backward repositioning or rejection. In the F03 reference, BE63 and BE64 are an explicit stacking test: if they can share a stable first layer side-by-side, that can be preferable to putting them one behind another and should be evaluated as a load-bearing alternative. Fixed left/right alternation is not a rule: each next unit should compare beside, behind, and above positions, including future support and center-of-gravity effects.

**Why:** The F02 reference uses a simple trailer and still shows a substantial rearward center offset. In real loading practice, the load would be moved further forward, so this is a diagnostic case for other platform types rather than a special exception.

**How to apply:** Evaluate center-of-gravity distance during placement and after any post-processing shift. Use axle-load limits when available; otherwise use the platform center as the baseline and show a clear warning for excessive deviation.
